Analogous colours
Colours that sit next to each other on the colour wheel, like orange and yellow.
Aperture
The adjustable opening inside the lens, written as an f-number. A smaller number means a wider opening.
Autofocus (AF-S, AF-C)
The camera focusing by itself, either once and then locking, or continuously while you hold the button.
Back light
Light coming from behind the subject towards the camera. It gives glowing outlines and silhouettes.
Barrel distortion
Straight lines bowing outwards near the edges of the frame, typical of wide-angle lenses.
Bokeh
The look of the out-of-focus parts of a picture, especially points of light in the background.
Bracketing
Taking several shots of the same scene at different exposures, to choose from or to blend later.
Chromatic aberration
Coloured fringes along high-contrast edges, caused by a lens bending colours by different amounts.
Clipping
Areas recorded as pure black or pure white, with no detail left to recover.
Colour grading
Giving a whole picture a colour mood in editing, for example cool shadows and warm highlights.
Colour temperature
The colour of light measured in kelvin. Low numbers are warm and orange, high numbers cool and blue.
Colour wheel
The hues arranged in a circle. Cameras and screens use a wheel built on red, green and blue light.
Complementary colours
Colours opposite each other on the colour wheel, like orange and blue. Side by side they make each other look stronger.
Converging verticals
Upright lines leaning towards each other when the camera tilts up or down.
Crop factor
How much a smaller sensor narrows the view compared with full frame, about 1.5 for APS-C.
Depth of field
The zone from near to far that looks acceptably sharp in a photo.
Diffraction
A slight overall softness at very small apertures such as f/22.
Exposure compensation
Telling the camera to make the picture brighter or darker than its meter suggests, in steps of a stop.
Exposure value (EV)
A single number for a combination of settings. One EV equals one stop.
Fill flash
A little flash used in daylight to soften shadows.
Flare
Stray light bouncing inside the lens, seen as haze or coloured shapes.
Focal length
Measured in millimetres, it sets how wide or narrow the view of a lens is.
Focus peaking
A display aid that outlines sharp edges in colour while you focus by hand.
Golden hour
The hour after sunrise and the hour before sunset, when light is low, warm and soft.
Hard light
Light from a small source, giving crisp shadows and high contrast.
Histogram
A graph of how many pixels have each brightness, from black on the left to white on the right.
Hue
The colour itself, such as red or blue, whatever its strength or brightness.
Hyperfocal distance
The closest focus distance that still keeps the far distance acceptably sharp.
ISO
How much the sensor’s signal is amplified. Higher ISO brightens the picture and adds noise.
Kelvin
The unit of colour temperature, written K.
Lens profile
Data an editing program uses to correct a particular lens’s distortion and vignetting.
Metering mode
Which part of the frame the camera’s light meter pays attention to.
Middle grey
The tone a light meter aims for, a grey that reflects 18% of the light.
ND filter
Neutral density filter. Dark grey glass that cuts light without changing colours.
Negative space
Empty area around a subject, used on purpose.
Noise
Grainy speckles and blotches of colour, most visible at high ISO and in dark areas.
Pincushion distortion
Straight lines bending inwards near the edges, typical of long zooms.
Polariser
A filter that cuts reflections and darkens blue skies when rotated.
Prime lens
A lens with a single focal length.
Raw file
The unprocessed data from the sensor, with the most room for editing.
Saturation
How strong a colour is, from plain grey to fully intense.
Shutter speed
How long the sensor collects light, written in seconds or fractions of a second.
Side light
Light from one side of the subject, which shows shape and texture.
Soft light
Light from a large source, giving gentle shadows and low contrast.
Stop
A doubling or halving of light.
Vibrance
An editing slider that strengthens muted colours more than strong ones and usually spares skin tones.
Vignetting
Darkening towards the corners of the frame.
White balance
The camera setting that makes white things look white in a given light.
Zoom lens
A lens whose focal length can be changed, such as 24–70 mm.
