About the club

The Conscious Frame is the photography club of Samskara Germany e.V. Here's who we are, how the club works, and the few rules that keep it friendly.

About us

At the Photography Club of Samskara Germany e.V., we believe that a photograph is not just an image. We view it as a perspective, a statement, a window into the world as someone else experiences it. This belief stems from the conviction that photographs are rarely made by a camera — they are made with the eye, the heart, and the mind. And in a world that often tends to flatten differences, we believe that perspective matters.

As a club, we welcome photographers of all backgrounds, skill levels, and styles. Whether you are a seasoned artist or picking up a camera for the first time, you will find in our club a space that is welcoming, non-hierarchical, and genuinely collaborative. We uphold the dignity of every member and are committed to maintaining a community free from discrimination, bigotry, and exclusion. The club is rooted in our organisational values of equality, inclusivity, secularism, and the championing of progressive thought.

We aspire, through our lenses, to document the lived experiences of life in Germany. Together, we shoot, learn, reflect, and grow — be it through workshops, exhibitions, photo walks, or open critique sessions.

Monthly themes, votes and polls

Every month a new theme goes up on the home page. Share one photo that fits before the month ends; interpret it however you like, and tick This is my entry for the monthly theme when you send it. The gallery collects every entry, and the theme archive keeps each month's theme with its entries and the ones members voted for most.

Each member has three votes a week for photos added to the gallery that week. Open a photo and tap the heart beside its title; tap it again to take the vote back. Each photo shows how many votes it has, as they come in; who voted for which photo stays secret. On Monday, the three photos with the most votes go on the home page. You can't vote for your own photos.

Now and then the club also runs a poll on a subject, shown on the home page with what to do and by when. Polls are about seeing what members enjoy, not about winning.

Life in Germany

Alongside the monthly themes, the club keeps long-term series that grow over months and years. The first is Life in Germany: everyday life here as we live it, from streets and kitchens to festivals, train platforms and winter mornings. When you share a photo, choose the series if it fits. Members find the series on the home page and in the gallery.

Photographer pages

Every member who shares photos has a page with all their photos, linked from their name under each photo and from the Photographers page. Add a line about yourself on the Share a photo page, if you like: where you live, what you photograph, how long you've been in Germany. Only members see these pages.

Events

Photo walks, workshops, exhibitions and meet-ups are on the Events page. Members tap I'm coming, so the organisers know who to expect, and see who else is going. Anyone can add an event to their calendar or share it on WhatsApp.

The exhibition

The Exhibition page shows a selection of members' photos to everyone, not only members. The club's admins choose them, but only from photos whose photographers have agreed: tick the box when you share a photo if you're happy for it to be shown there. Changed your mind? Tell an admin and the photo comes off the Exhibition page straight away.

Giving feedback

When you share a photo, choose Feedback welcome or Just sharing. The photo shows which one you chose. On a Just sharing photo, say what you enjoy and leave out suggestions; the gallery's Feedback welcome filter shows the photos where suggestions are wanted.

Members can comment on any photo in the gallery, under the photo when you open it. Like the photos, comments are only for members, and your name is shown with each one. You can delete your own comments at any time. If a comment breaks the rules below, tap Report under it: the admins will look at it, and the person who wrote it won't know who reported it.

Every comment here is written for the person who took the photo. Before you post, read it back as if it were about your own picture.

  • Start with what works. Name something you genuinely like, then make your suggestion.
  • Be specific. Say what you'd change and why.
  • Talk about the photo, not the person. No comments on anyone's skill, gear or age.
  • Leave room for taste. “I would try…” works better than “You should…”.

Your photos stay yours

You keep full copyright of everything you share. Posting here only gives the club permission to show your photo to its members, and, if you tick the box for it, in the public exhibition. The gallery itself is private: people need an invite code to see it, and it isn't listed by search engines. Only share photos you took yourself, and never repost or edit another member's photo without asking them first. More in the privacy policy.

What you can post

Any genre is welcome, from landscapes and street to macro, abstract and phone snapshots. Mark AI-generated or composited images as such. People who can be clearly identified should have agreed to be photographed.

Joining

Membership is free. Send us a short note with your name and, if you like, a link to a few of your photos. We'll reply with your personal invite code.

With your code you can see the members' gallery, comment and vote, and send photos from your phone on the Share a photo page. A club admin checks each photo before it appears in the gallery, and you can see on that page whether it's been approved.

Questions

Who can see my photos?

Club members, signed in with their invite code. Visitors to the site only see photos in the exhibition, and a photo only goes there if you agreed when you shared it.

Can I write in Malayalam?

Yes. Titles, descriptions and comments can be in Malayalam, English or both. Add a Malayalam keyboard to your phone: on Android, in Gboard, go to Settings > Languages > Add keyboard > Malayalam. Its transliteration layout turns English letters into Malayalam as you type: “namaskaram” becomes നമസ്കാരം. On iPhone, go to Settings > General > Keyboard > Keyboards > Add New Keyboard > Malayalam. Switch keyboards with the globe key.

Do I need a proper camera?

No. Plenty of members shoot mostly on their phones.

How many photos can I share?

Up to five a week. Fewer, better photos tend to get more thoughtful comments.

Someone left a comment that upset me. What can I do?

Tap Report under the comment. The admins see the report, with your reason if you add one, and can remove the comment. You can also contact the club through the Samskara Germany e.V. website.
